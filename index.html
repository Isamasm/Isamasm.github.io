I've always been fascinated by how systems work under the hood—how they're deployed, configured, and connected to create something reliable and scalable.

My core interests lie in system administration and infrastructure, especially when it comes to Linux, networking, virtualization, and server management. There's nothing more satisfying to me than rolling up my sleeves, building something from scratch, and troubleshooting it until it works just right.

A big part of my learning comes from homelabbing and experimentation. I love taking old or spare hardware and turning it into something useful—whether it's a virtualization node, a network appliance, or a small-scale cluster. Replicating real-world IT setups at home, like running multiple VMs, managing services, or setting up networks, helps me understand how these systems behave in practice. It's one thing to read about DNS, DHCP, or VPNs, but actually configuring them and seeing the results is what really cements my understanding.

Right now, I'm especially drawn to Kubernetes and lightweight distributions like k3s. The idea of orchestrating containers across multiple machines, managing workloads, and building resilient infrastructure is incredibly exciting. I want to dive deep into how clusters are built, how networking and storage work within them, and how to turn a collection of machines into a unified, reliable platform. My dream project is to set up a k3s homelab cluster on older hardware— it's the perfect way to combine my interests in Linux, networking, virtualization, and automation.

Looking ahead, I see DevOps as the next step in my journey. I'm eager to move beyond traditional sysadmin tasks and explore automation, infrastructure as code, and cloud-native technologies. Tools like Docker, Terraform, Ansible, and CI/CD pipelines are on my radar, and I'm particularly curious about how they fit into modern IT workflows. I don't see myself as a DevOps expert yet, but I'm determined to get there by strengthening my foundations in infrastructure and gradually adopting these practices.

I also use scripting and programming as tools to make infrastructure management smoother. Python and Bash are my go-to languages for automation, and I'm starting to explore C to better understand how Linux and operating systems work at a lower level. Git is another essential tool in my toolkit—it's not just about writing code, but about versioning, collaborating, and managing configurations efficiently.

At the end of the day, I'm all about learning by doing. Breaking things, fixing them, and iterating until everything works is how I grow. My goal is to keep building, experimenting, and turning those experiments into reliable, production-like setups. Whether it's tweaking a server, setting up a new service, or automating a repetitive task, I thrive on the hands-on experience.
